# Editorial guidance

Write in clear US English with a calm, practical, technically credible voice. Develop each point through connected reasoning that explains the decision, its dependencies, and its consequences.

Move directly into the subject. Do not announce what the article will cover or describe the writing as a journal, recap, or set of notes. Use headings for substantive ideas, not the writer's intention.

Use full sentences and natural paragraphs. Avoid slogans, hype, invented personal experience, cultural stereotypes, and repeated first-person framing. Hypothetical situations must be clearly hypothetical.

Keep explanations concrete. Describe the operational constraint before prescribing a control. Explain how access, ownership, recovery, or evidence changes the decision. Do not treat scanning as the complete solution to credential risk.

Keep claim boundaries beside the claim. Distinguish exposure from misuse, a failed scan from a clean result, alert closure from verified invalidation, and missing evidence from evidence of absence. State scope, dates, denominators, and uncertainty whenever they materially affect the conclusion.

Use primary sources for technical claims. A product capability, planning sequence, or local demonstration is not an external standard, a product comparison, or proof of production effectiveness.
